RPE TO VELOCITY CORRELATION — RESEARCH SUMMARY
================================================
Compiled from peer-reviewed literature for use in velocity-based RPE estimation.


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. BRYAN MANN VBT TABLE (Practitioner Framework, ~2010s)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The most widely used practitioner reference. Derived from a large collegiate
athlete coaching dataset, not a controlled peer-reviewed study. Lift-agnostic
(primarily squat-derived). Uses mean/average velocity.

  RPE | RIR | Mean Velocity (m/s)
  ----|-----|--------------------
   10 |  0  | < 0.30
    9 |  1  | 0.30 – 0.50
    8 |  2  | 0.50 – 0.75
    7 |  3  | 0.75 – 1.00
    6 |  4  | 1.00 – 1.30
    5 | 5+  | > 1.30

Note: Some experienced lifters grind to as low as 0.15 m/s at true max effort.
This table is NOT calibrated separately for bench press or deadlift.

Sources: Mash Elite Performance, GymAware, ZeroSumGains Blog


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
2. HELMS ET AL. 2017 — KEY PEER-REVIEWED STUDY
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Citation: Helms ER et al. "RPE and Velocity Relationships for the Back Squat,
Bench Press, and Deadlift in Powerlifters."
Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research, 31(2), 292–297.
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27243918/

Sample: 15 competitive powerlifters (12M, 3F), mean age 28.4 ± 8.5 years.
Worked up to 1RM on each lift in separate sessions.

MINIMUM VELOCITY THRESHOLD (MVT) AT 1RM (RPE ~9.6–9.7):

  Lift         | ACV at 1RM (m/s) | RPE at 1RM
  -------------|------------------|------------
  Back Squat   | 0.23 ± 0.05      | 9.6 ± 0.5
  Bench Press  | 0.10 ± 0.04      | 9.7 ± 0.4
  Deadlift     | 0.14 ± 0.05      | 9.6 ± 0.5

CORRELATION COEFFICIENTS:
  ACV vs. RPE:   r = −0.79 to −0.87 (strong inverse)
  ACV vs. %1RM:  r = −0.90 to −0.92 (very strong inverse)
  %1RM vs. RPE:  r =  0.88 to  0.91 (very strong)

Important: This study used competitive powerlifters. Their 1RM velocities are
significantly lower than the general trained population (see Section 5).
The authors recommend building individual velocity-load profiles rather than
relying on group means.

3. BACK SQUAT LOAD-VELOCITY TABLE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Source: Sanchez-Medina et al., PMC6226068
Sample: 80 strength-trained male athletes

  %1RM | MV (m/s)     | MPV (m/s)    | Approx RPE
  -----|--------------|--------------|------------
   40% | 1.19 ± 0.08  | 1.28 ± 0.08  | ~5
   50% | 1.08 ± 0.07  | 1.14 ± 0.07  | ~5–6
   60% | 0.95 ± 0.07  | 1.00 ± 0.07  | ~6
   70% | 0.82 ± 0.06  | 0.84 ± 0.06  | ~7
   80% | 0.67 ± 0.04  | 0.68 ± 0.04  | ~8
   90% | 0.50 ± 0.03  | 0.51 ± 0.03  | ~9
  100% | 0.32 ± 0.03  | 0.32 ± 0.03  | ~10

MVT range at 1RM: 0.24–0.37 m/s (individual spread).
Regression: Load (%1RM) = –5.961 × MPV² – 50.71 × MPV + 117.0  (R² = 0.954)

Sample: 120 strength-trained males. R² = 0.98 for MPV vs. %1RM.

Source: Benavides-Ubric et al., PMC7429441
Sample: Trained subjects

  %1RM | MV (m/s)     | MPV (m/s)    | Approx RPE
  -----|--------------|--------------|------------
   40% | 1.02 ± 0.09  | 1.09 ± 0.12  | ~5
   50% | 0.91 ± 0.08  | 0.96 ± 0.10  | ~5–6
   60% | 0.80 ± 0.07  | 0.83 ± 0.08  | ~6–7
   70% | 0.68 ± 0.06  | 0.71 ± 0.07  | ~7
   80% | 0.57 ± 0.05  | 0.58 ± 0.05  | ~8
   90% | 0.45 ± 0.04  | 0.45 ± 0.04  | ~9
  100% | 0.33 ± 0.04  | 0.33 ± 0.04  | ~10

Regression: Load (%1RM) = –80.188 × MV + 124.929

Note: The general trained deadlift MVT (0.33 m/s) is much higher than the Helms
powerlifter sample (0.14 m/s). Large discrepancy likely due to population
difference (general trained vs. competitive powerlifters) and equipment
(straps, deadlift shoes alter the velocity profile).


────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
6. TRAINED VS. UNTRAINED / POWERLIFTER DIFFERENCES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
One of the most practically important findings — frequently overlooked.

  Population                        | Squat MVT  | Bench MVT  | Deadlift MVT
  ----------------------------------|------------|------------|-------------
  General/novice lifters            | 0.30–0.34  | 0.15–0.16  | 0.25–0.33
  Competitive powerlifters (Helms)  | 0.23±0.05  | 0.10±0.04  | 0.14±0.05
  Female recreational (Balsalobre)  | 0.23–0.25  | ~0.19      | —

Key finding: Experienced powerlifters have significantly lower MVTs.
Applying general-population velocity tables to powerlifters will consistently
UNDERESTIMATE the intensity being trained at a given velocity.
The bench press difference is most extreme: 0.16 m/s (general) vs. 0.10 m/s
(powerlifter) — a 40% difference at true max effort.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
8. KEY CAVEATS AND LIMITATIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. NO UNIVERSAL TABLE IS VALIDATED FOR ALL LIFTERS.
   The load-velocity relationship must be individually profiled for accurate
   prescription. Group means can be off by 0.10–0.15 m/s for any individual.

2. MVT VARIES DRAMATICALLY BY LIFT.
   Bench ~0.10–0.16 m/s vs. squat ~0.23–0.32 m/s vs. deadlift ~0.14–0.33 m/s.
   Never apply one lift's velocity thresholds to another.

3. POWERLIFTERS MOVE SLOWER AT MAX EFFORT THAN GENERAL POPULATION.
   Applying general-population tables to competitive powerlifters overestimates
   intensity at a given velocity.

4. WOMEN MAY HAVE SLIGHTLY LOWER MVTs.
   Squat: ~0.23 vs. ~0.26 m/s; Bench: ~0.19 vs. ~0.21 m/s (women vs. men).

5. DAY-TO-DAY VARIABILITY IS REAL.
   Velocity at a given %1RM fluctuates due to fatigue, sleep, and readiness.
   This is actually the primary use case for VBT — autoregulation, not static
   look-up tables.

6. INDIVIDUAL r² BETWEEN VELOCITY AND PERCEIVED RIR IS ONLY ~0.30.
   (PeerJ 2025 study, n=2,972 measurements). Velocity predicts perceived effort
   poorly for many individuals, despite strong group-level correlations.
   RPE-velocity mapping is most reliable near failure (RPE 9–10) and becomes
   progressively less reliable at lower intensities (RPE 6–7).

7. MULTI-REP SETS COMPLICATE EVERYTHING.
   The tables above apply to single-rep efforts. In multi-rep sets, velocity
   declines across reps; RPE-velocity mapping is governed by velocity loss (%)
   relative to the first rep, not absolute velocity alone.

8. MV vs. MPV DISTINCTION MATTERS AT LIGHTER LOADS.
   At lighter loads, mean velocity (MV) and mean propulsive velocity (MPV)
   diverge because there is a deceleration phase; MPV excludes this and is more
   stable for load estimation at submaximal intensities. At ≥80% 1RM, they
   converge and the distinction becomes less important.
